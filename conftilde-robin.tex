\section{The Strata of  \texorpdfstring{$\wt{C}_A$}{the big configuration space}}

For a finite set $S$, denote by $|S|$ the number of elements in $S$. 

Let $A$ be a finite set.  In this section we describe the Strata of $\wt{C}_A$. 

\subsection{The combinatorics}
\begin{dfn}An {\it $A$-labeled tree} consists of
\begin{itemize}
\item
a tree $T$, meaning a connected acyclic graph, with vertex set $V(T)$ and edge set $E(T)$;

\item 
a distinguished vertex $r\in V(T)$ called the {\em root} (which may have any positive valence); 

%\item every vertex $v\in V(T)$ is associated with a subset of $A$; call it the ``points label'', denoted by $lp(v)$;

\item 
a map $ls: V(T) \to \{\R^d, \pi_1, \pi_2,\pi_\rI \}$, where we call $ls(v)$ the space label of $v$; and

\item
a bijection to $A$ from the set of {\em leaves} $v$ of $T$ such that $ls(v)=\R^d$, where a leaf is a non-root vertex of valence 1.  Thus such leaves are $A$-labeled, and we have an injective map from the set of all leaves $L(T)$ to $A\sqcup \{\pi_1, \pi_2,\pi_\rI \}$.

\item
We set further terminology:  For two distinct vertices $v, w\in V(T)$, if the unique path from $r$ to $v$ passes through $w$, %we say $w$ is an {\it ancestor} $v$ and $v$ is a {\it descendant} of $w$ (despite drawing the root at the bottom of the tree), 
we say $v$ lies {\em above} $w$ or is an {\em ancestor} of $w$, and we write $v>w$, with $<$ defining a partial order on $V(T)$.  If $v$ and $w$ are also adjacent, then we say $w$ is the {\it parent} of $v$ and $v$ is a {\it child} of $w$, and we denote the set of children of $v$ by 
%$cld(v)$; 
$\Ch(v)$.
For any $e\in E(T)$, we say the vertex adjacent to $e$ and closer to $r$ is {\it just below} $e$ and denote it by $v_-(e)$, and we say the vertex adjacent to $e$ and further away from $r$ is {\it just above} $e$ and denote it by $v_+(e)$.  
We view an edge $e$ as directed from $v_-(e)$ to $v_+(e)$.

%to every non-leaf vertex $v\in V(T)$ we associate one of the four labels $\R^d, \pi_1, \pi_2,\pi_\rI$ called the{\em space label} of $v$ and denoted by $ls(v)$; 

\end{itemize}
We require the following conditions:
\begin{itemize}
\item if $v\in V(T)$ is such that $ls(v)=\R^d$, then 
%$|cld(v)|+|lp(v)|\ge2$. 
$|\Ch(v)|\neq 1$; and

\item either both $\pi_1, \pi_2$ are associated to exactly one vertex, and no vertex is associated to $\pi_\rI$;

or $\pi_\rI$ is associated to exactly one vertex, and no vertex is associated to $\pi_1$ or $\pi_2$.
%\item if $v\neq w \in V(T)$, then $lp(v)\cap lp(w)=\emptyset$;
%\item $\bigsqcup_{v\in V(T)}lp(v)=A$;
\end{itemize}

Denote by $\cT(A)$ the set of $A$-labeled trees. 
\end{dfn}

As a rooted tree, any $A$-tree $T$ has the property that the map $e \mapsto v_+(e)$ is a bijection from edges of $T$ to non-root vertices of $T$.

The strata of $\wt{C}_A$ will be labeled by elements of $\cT(A)$.

Let $T_1$ be the maximal subtree whose root is the vertex $r_1 \neq r$ closest to $r$ with $ls(r_1)\neq \R^d$ and such that any vertex with space label other than $\R^d$ has no children in $T_1$.  If there are two candidates for $r_1$, we may choose $r_1$ so that $ls(r_1)=\pi_1$.  It is possible that $T_1$ is a single vertex, and if $ls(r)=\pi_\rI$, then $T_1=\varnothing$.  
Let $T_2$ be the maximal subtree whose root $r_2$ is the remaining vertex with $ls(r_2)\neq \R^d$; $T_2$ may be a single vertex.  If some vertex is labeled by $\pi_\rI$, then $T_2$ is empty.  

Now $T=T_0\cup T_1\cup T_2$, where each union is along a single vertex $r_i$ such that $ls(r_i) \neq \R^d$.   

Each $T_i$ has an injection from a subset $A_i \subset A$ to its set of leaves, with $A=A_0 \sqcup A_1 \sqcup A_2$.
Each $T_i$ is not quite an $A_i$-tree because it is possible that only one of the space labels $\pi_1$ and $\pi_2$ appears in the tree.
%Each $T_i$ is not quite an $A$-tree because the leaf labeling is an injection $L(T) \to A\sqcup \{\pi_1, \pi_2,\pi_\rI \}$ whose image may not contain all of $A$ and whose image may contain only one of $\pi_1$ and $\pi_2$.
Nonetheless, we define $S_{T_i}$, with $i=0,1,$ or $2$, in the same manner that we defined $S_T$ for $T\in \mathcal{T}_A$.  

For $i=0,1,$ and $2$, we will define maps
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:multiple-smoothing}
S_{T_i} \x \prod_{e \in E^c(T_i)} [0,\epsilon_e) \to F(ls(r_i))^{A_i \sqcup R_i}
%\wt{C}_{A_i \sqcup R_i}(ls(r_i))
\end{equation}
where $F(ls(r_i))=\R^d$ if $ls(r_i)= \R^d$ and $F(ls(r_i))$ is the fiber of the bundle $ls(r_i)$ containing the configuration $\bx_{r_i}$ otherwise, and where
\[
R_i=\{ r_j : j\in\{0,1,2\} \text{ and } r_j \text{ lies above } r_i \text{ but above no other } r_k \}.
\]




A point in $S_{T_i}$ is given by $\big( \bx_v \big)_{v \in V(T_i)}\in \prod_{v \in V(T_i)} C_{\Ch(v)}(ls(v))$.
For $a \in A_i \sqcup R_i$, let $x_{v,a}$ be the point in the configuration $\bx_v$ corresponding to the child of $v$ that lies below the leaf $a$.

Writing $\star_{i,a}=x_{r_i,a}$ for $i=0,1,$ or $2$, we define the map \eqref{eq:multiple-smoothing} by 
\[
\left( \big( \bx_v \big)_{v \in V(T_i)}, \ (t_e)_{e \in E^c(T_i)} \right) 
\mapsto
\left(
\exp_{\star_{i,a}} \left( \sum_{\substack{v < a\\ v \neq r_i}} \left( \prod_{\substack{e \in E^c(T_i)\\ v_+(e)<a}} t_e \right) x_{v,a} \right) \right)_{a \in A_i \sqcup R_i}.
\]
%We can view the resulting element of $\prod_{i=0}^2 F(ls(r_i))^{A_i \sqcup R_i}$ as an element of $\wt{C}_{A_i \sqcup R_i}(ls(r_i))$.  

Let $T'$ be the tree obtained from $T$ by contracting all the contractible edges in $T_0$, $T_1$, and $T_2$.  
We have  $0 \leq |E^c(T')| \leq 2$.  If $|E^c(T')|=0$, then we take $\psi_T$ to be the map \eqref{eq:multiple-smoothing}.  If $|E^c(T')|>0$, then each contractible edge in $T'$ is directed either 
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] from the root $r$ with $ls(r)=\R^d$ to a vertex $v$ with $ls(v)\neq \R^d$, or 
\item[(ii)] from a vertex with space label $\pi_i$ to a vertex with space label $\pi_{3-i}$.
\end{itemize}
In cases (i) and (ii), we will apply the smoothing of a single node given in Definition \ref{D:smooth-one-node}, parts (c) and (d) respectively.  

If $|E^c(T')|=1$, this allows us to define $\psi_T$ without ambiguity.

If $|E^c(T')|=2$, we consider whether the order of contracting the two edges matters.  In this case, either one or both edges is joined to the root.  If only one is, the two smoothings affect disjoint disks in $ls(r)$; thus their composition can be expressed as a product of two maps and the order does not matter.  If both contractible edges in $T'$ are joined to the root, then the first contraction gives a smoothing as in Definition \ref{D:smooth-one-node} (c) while the second gives a smoothing as in Definition \ref{D:smooth-one-node} part (d).  Regardless of the order of edge contractions, the leaves adjacent to the root in $T'$ become (the same) configuration points in the equatorial region of $E_\rI$, so the order does not matter.
